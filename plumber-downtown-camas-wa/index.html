Plumbing in the Historic Core of Camas

Downtown Camas is the historic center of the city. The LaCamas Colony Company chose the townsite in 1883 for a paper mill that needed lake water to run its machines, and Camas was incorporated as a town in 1906. A fire in 1923 destroyed many of the wooden downtown buildings, and the district was rebuilt mainly in brick.

Today the blocks around downtown mix shops, restaurants and offices with homes. Local real estate guides describe older homes, restored properties, cottages and bungalows depending on the street, with parks, the library and local events close by.

What a Mixed Residential and Commercial Area Means for Plumbing

This is general plumbing information, not a claim about what is under any particular street. An older house and a storefront next door can share a block and still have very different needs. What matters is the age of the work, who touched it last, and whether anyone knows where the lines run.

  • Buying or remodeling an older home. A sewer camera inspection shows the condition of the line before you commit, and it is one of the few ways to see inside a pipe you cannot dig up.
  • Slow or repeat drains. A clog that keeps returning is a reason for drain cleaning with a look at why it happens.
  • Restaurants, shops and offices. Heavy fixture use and grease are handled on our commercial plumbing page.
  • Renovations. Moving a sink, adding a bath or opening a wall is plumbing installation work that needs a permit and an inspection.

Old walls, new fixtures

If you are updating a kitchen or bath in an older building, ask what the existing supply and drain lines are made of before the walls close. A plumber can look at the lines while they are open, which is cheaper than finding out later.

Questions Before You Buy or Renovate Near Downtown

Many of the plumbing questions here come up around a sale or a remodel rather than an emergency. A buyer wants to know about the main drain. A renovator wants to know where the shutoff is. A shop owner wants to know whether a new sink or dishwasher can share a line. Those conversations go better with a camera look and a clear list of what is in the walls.

City Sewer and Water Information for Downtown Addresses

If a leak has pushed up a water bill, the city's rules may help. Camas Municipal Code 13.44.030 provides for a leak adjustment on water bills, so ask the Public Works Water Division whether a repaired leak at your property qualifies.

Before you hire anyone for downtown plumbing work, look the business up on the Washington Labor and Industries contractor verification tool, and keep the registration details with your permit paperwork.

Plumbing Questions Downtown Camas Property Owners Ask

Where does downtown Camas start and end?

There is no single official boundary for the name. This page treats downtown as the historic center of the city and does not draw an exact line, so give us your street address when you call.

Should I get a sewer camera inspection before buying an older home?

It is a common step for any older property, because it shows the line from the house to the street. Our sewer camera inspection page explains what a camera visit shows.

Can you help a small business with plumbing?

Ask when you call. Our commercial plumbing page lists the kinds of shop, restaurant and office work we handle, and we say plainly what we can take on.

Does a renovation downtown need a permit?

Often it does. Moving a fixture or adding a bath is permitted work under the 2021 Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and the City of Camas Building Division can tell you what applies to your address before the walls open.

Nearby Parts of Camas

Uphill from the center of town is Prune Hill, and Lacamas Lake sits to the north, home to Lacamas Shores. The Camas service area page has the whole city.
