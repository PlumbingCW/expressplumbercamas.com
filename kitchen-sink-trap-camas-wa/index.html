Kitchen Sink Plumbing Trap: A Guide for Camas, WA Homes

The curved pipe under your kitchen sink does more than catch lost rings. Here is how it works, how to clean it and when it needs more than that.

By the Camas Express Plumbing team · Updated November 14, 2026

What a Sink Trap Does

The U-shaped or S-shaped pipe under your kitchen sink is called a P-trap. It holds a small amount of water that blocks sewer gas from rising up the drain into the kitchen, and it catches heavy debris before it travels down the line.

That standing water is the reason a dry trap smells. If a sink is not used for weeks, the water evaporates and gas can come up, so run water in unused sinks from time to time.

Why a Kitchen Trap Clogs

  • Grease. It cools in the trap and coats the walls until water barely passes.
  • Food particles. Rice, pasta and coffee grounds settle in the bend.
  • Soap scum. Combines with grease to form a hard paste.
  • Dropped objects. Rings, bottle caps and utensils lodge in the trap.

The trap is designed to be the first spot to catch these things, which is why the trap is usually where a kitchen clog is found.

How to Clean a Kitchen Sink Trap

  1. Place a bucket under the trap and clear the cabinet.
  2. Loosen the slip nuts on either side of the trap by hand or with pliers.
  3. Lower the trap into the bucket, tip out the contents and scrub it.
  4. Check the arm that leads into the wall for buildup, and clear it with a small brush.
  5. Reassemble the trap with the washers seated and tighten the nuts by hand, then a quarter turn more.
  6. Run water and check for leaks.

If water still drains slowly after the trap is clean, the blockage is deeper in the line, and our drain cleaning service reaches it. If the problem keeps returning, hydro jetting cleans the pipe wall.

Common Mistakes With Kitchen Traps

Over-tightening plastic nuts cracks them and causes slow drips. Using too much plumber's tape on a slip joint can also stop it from sealing. Mixing metal and plastic parts without the right washers invites leaks.

Pouring boiling water or a chemical cleaner into a clogged trap rarely solves it. Hot water softens grease briefly and sends it farther down the line, and chemicals burn the next person who opens the trap.

Camas Kitchen Lines and the STEP System

Some Camas homes connect to the City of Camas STEP sewer system, where the property owner maintains the line from the house to the tank and the city owns the tank, the pump and the control panel. The city asks owners not to use additives and says flushable wipes are not suitable, so avoid trap and drain additives and keep fats out of the kitchen drain.

Water in Camas comes from groundwater wells and is treated with sodium hydroxide to reduce copper corrosion. For kitchen traps, the more practical concern is the grease and food waste that goes down the drain, and the habits in this guide work for any home.

The Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) requires a trap on each fixture drain, which is why removing one to chase a clog means putting a trap back.

When to Call a Plumber

Call a plumber if more than one fixture is draining slowly, if the trap leaks after you reassemble it or if water backs up into a second sink. These signs point to a clog beyond the trap, and the fix is a cable or a camera, not another round with a wrench.

Double Sinks, Disposals and Dishwashers

A double kitchen sink usually shares one trap through a tailpiece and a continuous waste, and a garbage disposal adds a second outlet that must connect above the trap. The dishwasher drain hose attaches to the disposal or a tailpiece with a looped hose, so water cannot flow back into the machine.

If water from the dishwasher shows up in the sink, or the sink fills when the dishwasher runs, the clog is usually between the trap and the main line. A garbage disposal repair visit also checks the connection.

Parts That Cause Slow Leaks Under the Sink

  • A cracked slip nut that weeps when water flows.
  • A washer set crooked inside a joint.
  • A tailpiece that sits too short, so the joint is under strain.
  • Corroded metal trap arms that pit through.
  • Cabinet clutter that bumps pipes and loosens joints.

A slow leak under the sink can rot the cabinet floor and grow mold before you notice. Put a small dry paper towel under the trap each month. If it comes up wet, tighten the joint or replace the part, and if you cannot find the source, call a plumber.

Tools and Tips for Working Under the Sink

Slip-joint pliers, a bucket and a flashlight handle most traps. Put a towel in the cabinet and wear gloves, since the sludge in a clogged trap is unpleasant. Photograph the pipes before you take them apart so you can put them back the same way.

Plastic parts are easy to cross-thread, so start every nut by hand and stop when it is snug. Replace worn washers while the trap is out, because a washer costs very little and a cabinet floor does not.

If the trap is metal and corroded, replace it with a new plastic or chrome one. That avoids the next leak and takes only a few minutes once the old one is out.

Key Takeaways

  • The trap holds water that blocks sewer gas, so a wet trap is a healthy trap.
  • Grease, soap scum and food are the usual causes of kitchen clogs.
  • Hand-tighten slip nuts and check for leaks after you reassemble.
  • A clog beyond the trap needs a cable or a camera, not more cleaner.

Questions About Kitchen Traps in Camas

Should I use chemical drain cleaner on a kitchen sink in Camas?

It is best avoided, and the City of Camas asks owners on its STEP system not to use additives. A manual clean or a cable is safer.

Why does my kitchen sink smell?

A dry trap, food residue in the trap or the disposal, or a venting issue can all cause odors.

How often should I clean the trap?

Most households do it when the drain slows or the sink smells, and a yearly check is reasonable.

Is it normal for water to sit in the trap?

Yes. That water is what blocks sewer gas, so a trap that holds water is working.
