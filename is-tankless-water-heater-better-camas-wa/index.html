Is a Tankless Water Heater Better in Camas, WA?

Tankless is better for some Camas homes and worse for others. Here is how to tell which camp you are in before you spend the money.

By the Camas Express Plumbing team · Updated November 14, 2026

The Short Answer

A tankless water heater is better when you want a continuous supply of hot water, have limited space and can support the gas or electric load it needs. A tank is better when you want a simple install, a lower upfront price or a setup that works through a power outage.

Neither is universally better. The right pick depends on the number of people in your home, how many fixtures run at once, your fuel and the condition of your gas line or electrical panel.

How Hot Water Supply Differs

A tank stores a fixed amount of hot water. When it runs out, you wait for it to recover. A tankless unit heats water as it flows, so a long shower does not drain a tank, but the unit has a flow limit, and if two showers and a dishwasher start at once it can run out of capacity.

This is why we count fixtures before recommending anything. A household that runs one shower at a time can be happy with a small tankless unit, while a larger household may need a bigger model or two units.

Space, Fuel and Installation

  • Space. Tankless units hang on a wall and free up floor space.
  • Gas supply. Gas models often need a larger gas line than a tank.
  • Electric supply. Electric tankless models need high-amp circuits that some panels cannot support.
  • Venting. Gas tankless units use specific venting that may differ from the old flue.
  • Water quality. Scale in the heat exchanger shortens life if it is not flushed.

Our tankless water heater page explains how we size units, and if you want a conventional swap, see water heater replacement.

Maintenance and Lifespan

A tankless unit does not have a tank to corrode, and many manufacturers advertise a long service life. In practice, scale and neglected maintenance shorten it, and the flush schedule is what matters most. A tank needs a sediment flush and an anode rod check, which are simple jobs when done on time.

Camas Water Chemistry and Tankless Heaters

The City of Camas Public Works Water Division draws from groundwater wells and adds chlorine, fluoride and sodium hydroxide, which it uses to reduce copper pipe corrosion. Its 2025 Consumer Confidence Report does not state a hardness figure, so you cannot assume your water is soft or hard from the report alone.

A simple hardness test strip from a hardware store tells you what you have at your own tap. If the water is hard, plan to descale a tankless unit on the manufacturer's schedule, and consider a softener if scale is heavy. If the water is soft, tankless maintenance becomes lighter.

Venting and gas piping for a gas tankless unit follow the maker's installation instructions and the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted in Washington.

When a Tank Is the Smarter Pick

A tank is a good choice if your gas line is small, your electrical panel is full, the budget is tight or the heater is in a spot where a tankless venting run would be hard. A tank is also easier to replace in an emergency, because it is a like-for-like swap.

If you are replacing a failed tank on a deadline, a same-day swap is often the practical answer, and a tankless upgrade can follow later when you have time to plan it with a water heater installation visit.

What the Real Cost Comparison Looks Like

The sticker price of a tankless unit is higher, and the install often costs more because of the venting and supply upgrades. Over many years the running cost can be lower for some households, but a family that uses a lot of hot water may see little difference. The honest way to compare is to price both installs for your house and then look at your gas or power bill.

Resale matters too. Some buyers value the wall-mounted unit and the endless hot water, and others prefer the simplicity of a tank. Neither is wrong, so decide on how you live, not on a headline.

A Quick Decision Guide

  • Choose tankless if you have space limits, a large household with staggered use and a gas or electric supply that can handle it.
  • Choose a tank if budget, simplicity or a small gas line are the main concerns.
  • Choose a heat pump tank if you have electric service, open air and want lower power use.
  • Ask for a plumber's opinion if you are on the fence, because the house itself often decides.

Questions to Ask Any Installer

  • Will my gas line and meter handle the unit, or is an upgrade needed?
  • How will you vent it, and does the route need new penetrations?
  • What flow rate does this unit deliver at my winter temperature rise?
  • What maintenance does the maker require, and how often?
  • What happens to my hot water if the power goes out?

Clear answers tell you the installer has looked at your house and not just at a catalog. If the answer is a shrug, keep looking. We are glad to answer these on a call at (360) 371-6092 before you decide.

Key Takeaways

  • Tankless suits households that want continuous hot water and wall space.
  • A tank suits tight budgets, small gas lines and simple swaps.
  • Winter flow, not summer flow, sets the size of a tankless unit.
  • Test your water hardness before you decide, since scale drives tankless maintenance.

Questions About Tankless in Camas

Does the City of Camas report water hardness?

The city's 2025 water quality report does not list a hardness figure. Use a home test strip or ask the Public Works Water Division, and plan descaling if the water is hard.

Will a tankless heater save money?

It can lower standby heat loss, but the savings depend on how much hot water you use and what you pay for gas or power. Upfront cost and install work are higher.

Can I install tankless on my existing gas line?

Sometimes, but many units need a bigger line than a tank, so the line and the meter are checked first.

How long does a tankless unit take to deliver hot water?

The unit heats on demand, but the water still has to travel through the pipe, so long runs mean a wait. A recirculation line can help.
