Copper Pipe Repair in Camas, WA Homes: Leaks and Options

Copper lasts for decades, but it does fail, and the way it fails tells you what to do next. Here is how to read the signs.

By the Camas Express Plumbing team · Updated November 14, 2026

Why Copper Is Still a Common Choice

Copper has been a standard supply pipe for generations because it is durable, handles heat and does not break down in sunlight. Many homes in and around Camas have copper supply lines, and plenty of that pipe is still working well after decades.

Still, copper is not immortal. Water chemistry, flow speed, installation quality and temperature swings all change how long a given run lasts, and the way a copper pipe fails is a clue about the cause.

How Copper Pipe Fails

Pinhole leaks

Tiny leaks that spray a fine mist or leave a green or white crust, often caused by corrosion from the inside.

Pitting and erosion

Rough spots near fittings or bends where fast-moving water wears the wall thin.

Freeze splits

A long slit along the pipe after a hard freeze.

Failed joints

Leaks at soldered fittings from poor workmanship or age.

A single pinhole in a section of pipe is a repair. Several pinholes within a few feet, or a leak every year or two in different spots, are a sign the pipe wall is thinning throughout and replacing the run is the better answer.

Repair Options for a Leaking Copper Pipe

  • Slip coupling or compression fitting for a quick fix on a clean, straight section.
  • Soldered section replacement where a damaged length is cut out and new copper sweated in.
  • Push-fit fittings that connect copper to PEX or copper to copper without a flame.
  • Whole-run replacement when corrosion is widespread, covered under repiping.

Soldering inside a wall, near insulation or wood framing takes care. A plumber has the heat shields, the water control and the fire watch to do it safely, which is one reason wall leaks are not a good weekend project.

Water Chemistry and Copper

Water that is acidic or low in minerals can attack copper from the inside. Utilities treat their water to limit that, and it works well in most cases, but local conditions inside a house, such as hot water recirculation or poor grounding, can still cause trouble.

If the leaks keep coming, a plumber can look at the pipe pattern and at the water, and a full leak detection visit finds the ones you have not seen yet.

Camas Water Treatment and Copper Pipe

The City of Camas Public Works Water Division adds sodium hydroxide at its treatment plant to reduce corrosion of copper pipes, alongside chlorine and fluoride. Its 2025 Consumer Confidence Report shows lead and copper testing was compliant, which speaks to the water as it arrives and not to the age or condition of the pipes in an individual home.

That is a useful distinction. Treated water lowers the odds of copper pinholes citywide, but a house with 40-year-old copper, a hot water recirculation loop or a bad solder job can still have a failure. When you see blue-green stains or repeated leaks, look at your own pipes before you blame the supply.

Copper tube and fittings for water supply are made to recognized ASTM standards, and the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) lists approved materials for potable water.

Preventing Copper Leaks

Keep water pressure in a reasonable range with a pressure regulator, since high pressure stresses every joint. Insulate exposed copper in crawlspaces and exterior walls, and know where your main shutoff is. A moisture check under sinks and near the water heater twice a year catches slow leaks early.

What to Do While You Wait for a Repair

Shut off the water to the affected area, or the whole house if you cannot isolate it. A pinhole spraying a fine mist can soak insulation and drywall for hours, so catch the water with a bucket and place a towel to protect the floor.

Temporary repair clamps and wraps can slow a leak but they are not permanent, and they hide the real condition of the pipe. Treat them as a way to buy time, not a fix.

How a Plumber Decides Between Repair and Replacement

  • Count the leaks and how close together they are.
  • Look at the color and thickness of the pipe wall at the cut.
  • Check the age of the pipe and the fittings.
  • Consider how hard the run is to reach, because repeated access costs more than one bigger job.
  • Weigh the repair against the cost of repiping the run or the house.

The goal is a fix that lasts. A plumber who explains why they chose one option over another is giving you what you need to decide.

Signs Your Copper Pipes Are Aging Out

  • Green or white crust on pipes or at fittings.
  • Stains on ceilings or walls below bathrooms and kitchens.
  • Pinhole leaks that appear one after another.
  • Blue-green stains in sinks or tubs.
  • A drop in water pressure at several fixtures.

None of these alone is proof that a repipe is due, but together they build a picture. A plumber can look at accessible pipe, cut a small sample if needed and give you a read on how much life is left. That is a better basis for a decision than guessing.

If the pipe has plenty of life left, a spot repair is the sensible choice. If not, planning a repipe on your schedule beats an emergency in the middle of the night.

Questions About Copper Pipe in Camas

Does Camas treat its water to protect copper pipe?

Yes. The City of Camas adds sodium hydroxide at treatment to reduce copper pipe corrosion, according to its water quality report.

Is blue-green water a sign of a copper pipe problem?

Blue-green staining can be a sign that copper is dissolving into the water. It is worth a look by a plumber and, if you are concerned, a water test.

Can I repair a copper pipe without soldering?

Yes. Push-fit and compression fittings avoid a flame, and plumbers use them in tight places, though a soldered joint is still the permanent standard in many cases.

When should I repipe instead of repairing?

When leaks keep appearing in different places, the pipe is badly corroded or the pipe has been repaired repeatedly, a repipe is the practical choice.
