Emergency Faucet Repair in Camas, WA

A faucet that sprays under the sink or will not shut off is a small plumbing problem that can become a large water bill. Call (360) 371-6092 and we will help you stop the water and plan the repair.

Faucet Spraying or Stuck On? Quick Steps

Look under the sink for two small valves, one for hot and one for cold. Turn each clockwise until snug to stop the flow, and if the valves will not turn, shut off the main water valve to the house.

Place a bucket under any drip and towels in the cabinet. Then call, and tell us the brand if you know it and whether the leak is from the spout, the handle or underneath.

Faucet Repairs We Do Most

  • Dripping spouts from worn cartridges, washers or seats.
  • Leaks at the handle from worn seals or loose packing nuts.
  • Weak flow from a clogged aerator or mineral buildup.
  • Leaks under the sink from supply lines or stop valves.
  • Stuck or stiff handles that need a new cartridge.

If the faucet is old or no longer has parts, replacement is faster and the new one has a warranty from the maker. Stop valves that will not close can also be replaced, and the extra fixture work fits under our plumbing installation service.

Camas Water and Faucet Wear

The City of Camas pulls drinking water from groundwater wells and treats it with chlorine and fluoride, plus sodium hydroxide to reduce copper pipe corrosion. Its 2025 consumer confidence report does not list hardness, so we recommend a home test strip if you see white crust around faucet spouts.

Mineral crust on aerators and cartridges is the most common reason a faucet loses pressure. A cleaning often helps, and if the cartridge is scored or pitted, a new one stops the drip for good.

Faucet flow limits come from federal efficiency standards, and the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) covers how a faucet connects to supply and drain.

Repair or Replace? A Simple Rule of Thumb

If the faucet is less than ten years old, from a known brand and only drips, repair is usually worth it. If the finish is worn, the handle is loose throughout and parts are discontinued, a new faucet is a better use of money.

A replacement also gives you the chance to fix other things under the sink at the same visit, such as old supply lines and a stop valve that no longer closes. That avoids a second trip when the next small leak appears.

Care Tips for Faucets

Clean aerators every few months to prevent mineral buildup, and wipe the finish with a soft cloth rather than harsh cleaners. If a handle feels stiff, address it early, since forcing it can strip the cartridge and turn a small repair into a large one.

Check the supply lines under the sink once or twice a year for bulges, rust or moisture, and replace braided lines that look worn.

Why is my faucet dripping?
