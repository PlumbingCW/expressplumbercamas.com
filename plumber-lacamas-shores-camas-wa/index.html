Plumbing for Lakeside Homes

Lacamas Shores is a residential community on the southwest shore of Lacamas Lake in Camas, governed by the Lacamas Shores Homeowners' Association. Local news coverage describes it as a neighborhood of higher-end homes, and local real estate guides place it among the more scenic parts of Camas, with trails, parks and water access nearby.

This page does not publish a street map of the community. Homeowners within the association can use the notes below, and we will ask for your street address when you call.

Jobs That Come Up for Lakeside Homes

The points here are general plumbing information. They are not claims about any home or street in the community. Lake views, tall trees and quiet streets say nothing about the condition of the pipes, but they do shape how a plumbing visit goes.

  • Mystery water bill. If usage climbs and nothing looks wrong, leak detection traces the loss without opening walls first.
  • Hot water that has slowed down. A tank that rumbles, leaks or cannot keep up leads to a water heater repair check, or a replacement discussion if the unit is at the end of its life.
  • Outdoor and lake-side lines. Spigots, irrigation feeds and lines to a dock, shed or outbuilding see the weather first. See water line repair.
  • Fixtures. Dripping faucets and running toilets quietly add to the bill and are quick repairs. See faucet repair.

Check your HOA rules before exterior or visible work

Homeowners associations often have covenants that cover exterior changes, such as vents, equipment or digging. Read yours or ask the association before a plumbing job that changes how the outside of the home looks. It is a general tip, and the covenants differ from one community to the next.

Cold Weather and Outdoor Plumbing

Camas has seen what a hard freeze can do. The January 2024 storm froze pipes at Skyridge Middle School, and about 3,200 gallons of water spread across 22 classrooms. A single home sees the same freeze risk on a smaller scale, especially at outdoor spigots and exposed lines near the lake. Disconnecting hoses and shutting off outdoor lines before a freeze is a simple precaution.

Camas Water and the Rules for Plumbing Work

The city's water information does not report a hardness figure, so a simple home test is the way to learn whether scale is building up in your water heater and on fixtures.

Digging for a new outdoor line, a hose bib relocation or a dock water feed? Ask the Building Division whether the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) calls for a permit, and call 811 first, as Washington's underground utilities law (RCW 19.122) requires, so buried utilities are marked before the shovel goes in.

Plumbing Questions Lacamas Shores Homeowners Ask

Is Lacamas Shores part of the City of Camas?

Local news describes it as a neighborhood in Camas on the southwest shore of Lacamas Lake. If you are unsure whether your address is inside city limits, the City of Camas can confirm, and it decides which office handles plumbing permits.

Do you cover the whole community?

Call with your street address and we will tell you what we can do. This page does not claim to know the exact boundaries of the association.

How do I find a hidden leak without tearing out walls?

Leak detection starts with a meter check to see whether water is moving when nothing is on, then narrows the search with listening and testing tools.

Does the HOA have to approve plumbing work?

That depends on your covenants and on the work. Interior repairs rarely change the look of a home, but exterior vents, equipment or digging might. Ask the association if you are unsure.
