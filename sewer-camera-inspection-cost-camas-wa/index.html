Plumbing Camera Inspection Cost in Camas, WA: What Drives the Price

A camera inspection costs less than the problems it finds. Here is what makes one cost more or less, and how to get the most from it.

By the Camas Express Plumbing team · Updated November 14, 2026

What You Are Paying For

A sewer camera inspection puts a small waterproof camera on a flexible rod into your drain line and records what it sees. You are paying for the plumber's time, the equipment and, most of all, the interpretation. Anyone can watch a video of a pipe, but a plumber tells you whether what you see is a problem.

We do not post flat prices for camera work because the setup varies. We do tell you the price before the work begins, and the factors below are how we get there.

What Makes an Inspection Cost More or Less

Access to the lineA cleanout outdoors or in a basement makes entry simple. A toilet pull adds time.
Line lengthLonger runs take more time and cable.
Cleaning firstA blocked line has to be cleared before the camera can pass.
Locating the problemMarking the surface above a defect takes extra equipment and time.
Recording and reportA saved video and a written note are part of the plan.
TimingSame-day and after-hours requests are priced differently.

A clear line with an easy cleanout is the simplest job. A line that needs cleaning first or one that serves a long, winding run is more involved.

When a Camera Inspection Pays for Itself

  • Buying a home. A hidden collapse can cost far more than the inspection, and findings can be used in negotiation.
  • Recurring clogs. The video shows the real cause so you stop paying for clearing alone.
  • Planning a dig. Knowing where the line runs avoids hitting it.
  • Before a remodel. New bathrooms add load to old lines.
  • Insurance questions. A recorded condition can help document a claim.

If the camera shows a failure, the next step is a repair plan, and the sewer line repair page explains the options. If it shows only buildup, hydro jetting may be all you need.

How to Get the Most From the Visit

Make sure the cleanout is accessible, and tell the plumber about past backups, old repairs and trees near the line. Ask for the video file and notes, and ask the plumber to explain the findings in plain terms. If you are buying, ask whether the seller's side or yours covers any repair.

Camera Inspections and the City of Camas STEP System

In the City of Camas STEP system, the city owns the tank, the pump and the control panel, while the owner maintains the line from the house to the tank. A camera inspection shows the condition of the part you own, and for a purchase, it is worth asking the city about the tank that serves the property.

If a camera finds a problem that needs digging, Washington law requires a call to 811 first, and utilities mark lines at least two business days ahead. For permits on a repair, ask the Camas Building Division, which administers the state-adopted plumbing code.

When a camera finds a defect, the repair is measured against the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) standards for slope, joints and materials as adopted in Washington.

Reading a Camera Report Without Being Overwhelmed

A good plumber will name what you are seeing and tell you whether it matters. Roots at a joint, a belly that holds water and a cracked pipe each have a different urgency, and a clean line with a few rough joints is a normal result for an older home.

Ask for the footage, the distance to each finding and a plain written note. If you plan to negotiate a repair with a seller, those three items are the evidence you need.

Cost Versus the Price of Not Knowing

Sewer repairs can be among the larger plumbing bills a homeowner faces, and a camera inspection is among the smaller ones. Spending a little to learn the condition of the line before you commit to a purchase or a remodel is almost always sensible.

  • Compare the inspection price with the cost range of repairs, not with zero.
  • Use the findings to plan maintenance, since cleaning on a schedule can extend the life of an aging line.
  • Keep the video with your home records for the next owner or the next plumber.

Questions to Ask Before You Book

  • Is the video recorded, and will I get a copy?
  • Does the price cover cleaning the line if it is blocked?
  • Will you mark the location of any defect on the surface?
  • Do you explain the findings in writing or only on site?
  • What would the next step cost if we find a problem?

A plumber who answers these plainly is a plumber worth hiring. The inspection is not a sales pitch for a repair, and a good one tells you when nothing needs to be done.

Key Takeaways

  • Price depends on access, length, cleaning and locating, so ask for it before work starts.
  • Insist on recorded video and a plain written note.
  • An inspection costs far less than a surprise sewer repair.
  • The findings can support a price negotiation or a repair plan.

If you are still weighing whether to book, remember that the line is the one part of your plumbing you cannot see. A short visit turns a guess into a fact, and facts are what let you plan a repair, ask a seller for a credit or simply sleep better.

Not necessarily. A blocked line has to be cleared first, which we explain before work starts.

Can I use the camera video when buying a home in Camas?

Yes. The video documents the line and can support a repair request, a price discussion or a decision to walk away.

Is a camera inspection worth it for a newer home?

It can be, since builders sometimes leave debris in new lines and a camera shows it.
