Plumbing Close to Home

What Camas Homes Ask of a Plumber

Camas gets its drinking water from groundwater wells and city reservoirs, and the Public Works Water Division adds sodium hydroxide at treatment to keep copper pipes from corroding. That is good news for the pipes inside your walls, and it is also why a plumber who sees a pinhole copper leak asks about age and water chemistry before recommending a fix. If you are chasing a mystery bill, our leak detection visit starts at your meter.

Many homes here are also on the city's STEP sewer system, where a small pump tank sits between the house and the street. The city owns the tank, the pump and the control panel, and you own the line from the house to the tank. When a drain slows down, a drain cleaning call is the first step, and a sewer camera inspection shows whether the problem sits in your pipe or past it.

Cold snaps matter too. During the January 2024 storm, frozen pipes at Skyridge Middle School released about 3,200 gallons of water across 22 classrooms, a reminder that insulation and a known shutoff location are worth more than any repair. Our burst pipe repair page walks through what to do in the first minutes.

Water heaters round out the list. A tank past its prime leaks without warning, so water heater replacement is often a planned job rather than an emergency, and we size every tankless water heater to the fixtures it must serve at once. All of it is measured against the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted in Washington and enforced by the Camas Building Division.
