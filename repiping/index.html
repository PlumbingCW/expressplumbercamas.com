Whole-House and Partial Repiping in Camas, WA

Repeated leaks, rusty water and falling pressure can all point to supply pipes that have reached the end of their life. Call (360) 371-6092 and we will look at your plumbing and tell you whether a repair or a repipe makes more sense.

Signs Your Pipes May Need Replacing

Repeated leaks

A second pinhole in the same area is rarely the last one.

Discolored water

Rust or brown tint from the tap can mean corroding pipe.

Falling water pressure

Mineral buildup narrows older metal pipe.

Visible corrosion

Green crust on copper or flaking rust on steel.

One leak is a repair. Three leaks in a few years is a pattern, and a pattern is a repipe conversation. We look at the material, the age and the location before we recommend anything.

Repiping Options and How the Work Goes

A repipe replaces the supply lines that carry water to fixtures. Modern options include PEX and copper, and each has its own cost, install method and warranty from the maker.

  • Whole-house repipe replaces all supply lines, fixture by fixture.
  • Partial repipe replaces a failing section such as a bathroom or kitchen run.
  • Shutoff upgrades add valves so you can isolate fixtures.
  • Wall and ceiling access is planned to minimize patching.
  • Pressure test confirms the new lines before the walls close.

If one failed pipe needs immediate attention, our burst pipe repair service handles the emergency, and a long-term plan comes after. Water heater connections are replaced at the same time when it makes sense, see our installation page.

Repiping, Water Chemistry and Code in Camas

The City of Camas adds sodium hydroxide at treatment to reduce copper pipe corrosion, and its 2025 report shows lead and copper testing was compliant. That is about the city's water at the tap, and it does not change the condition of the pipes inside your walls, which a plumber still has to check.

Repipes are permitted work in most cases. The Camas Building Division enforces the state-adopted Uniform Plumbing Code, and the 2021 codes took effect on March 15, 2024, so we plan the permit into the schedule.

Repipes use materials approved under the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted in Washington, and a permit and inspection are the normal way to confirm the work meets that standard.

Living Through a Repipe

A repipe is disruptive but not endless. We work room by room, protect floors and furniture, cut access holes in drywall only where needed and restore water to the house as soon as we safely can.

Plan to move items away from walls, expect some dust and noise and know that drywall repair and paint are separate from the plumbing. A clear schedule and a daily conversation with the crew keep it manageable.

Questions to Ask Before You Repipe

  • Do I need a full repipe or only part of the house?
  • What material do you recommend and why?
  • How will you handle the drywall and patching?
  • What permits are required, and who handles them?
