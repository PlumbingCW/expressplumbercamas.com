PVC Plumbing Leak Repair: A Camas, WA Homeowner Guide

A dripping PVC joint is one of the easier leaks to fix and one of the easier ones to get wrong. Here is how to tell what you have.

By the Camas Express Plumbing team · Updated November 14, 2026

Where PVC Is Used in a Home

PVC is a white or cream plastic pipe common in drain lines, vents and in some cold water lines, irrigation and sump discharge. It is not used for hot water supply inside the house, where CPVC, PEX or copper is used.

Knowing what pipe you have matters, because the repair, the glue and the primer are specific. PVC and CPVC look alike, but the cement is different, and mixing them leaves a joint that looks fine and fails later.

How to Find the Source of a PVC Leak

Dry the pipe with a towel, then watch where it beads. Water often travels along a pipe before it drips, so the wet spot may be a few inches from the source. Wipe the joint and run water through it to see the first drop.

  • Check joints and couplings first, where the glue seam is.
  • Look for hairline cracks along the pipe wall, especially near a stress point.
  • Check the threaded adapters and cleanout plugs.
  • Look for a slip joint or compression fitting that has loosened.
  • If water appears in a wall or ceiling, call a plumber before cutting.

Repairing a PVC Leak Safely

A leak at a slip joint can often be tightened or resealed. A cracked pipe or a failed glued joint needs a section cut out and replaced with new pipe and couplings, using primer and cement made for PVC and the cure time on the can.

  1. Shut off the water supply and drain the line.
  2. Cut out the damaged section with a pipe cutter or fine saw.
  3. Deburr and dry-fit the new section and couplings.
  4. Apply primer and cement, push the parts together and hold them.
  5. Wait the full cure time before turning the water back on and test for leaks.

Temporary wraps and repair tape can slow a drip while you plan a repair, but they are not permanent. If you cannot find the leak or it is in a wall, a plumber with leak detection tools is the safer route, and our leak detection page explains the process.

Pressure Lines Versus Drain Lines

A drain leak spills when water flows, and a pressurized leak runs all the time. A pressurized PVC line, such as irrigation, leaks harder and can wash out soil, which is why a yard leak needs a quick shutoff. If the leak is on the water line to the house, see water line repair.

PVC, Freeze and Digging in Camas

Plastic pipe near the surface can crack in a hard freeze, and a cold snap like the one in January 2024 caused frozen-pipe damage at two Camas schools according to the Camas-Washougal Post-Record. Exposed PVC irrigation and hose lines should be drained and protected before the freeze.

Before digging to reach a buried PVC line, Washington requires a call to 811, and utilities must mark buried lines at least two business days ahead. A cracked plastic pipe is easy to hit with a shovel, so mark and check before you dig.

Pipe and fittings used in plumbing have to be approved under the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), so use pipe, primer and cement marked for the application.

Common Mistakes With PVC Repairs

The most common mistake is gluing a wet joint. PVC cement needs a dry, clean surface, and water in the joint blocks the bond. Dry the pipe fully, or use a mechanical coupling when you cannot get it dry.

Another is skipping primer. Primer softens the plastic so the cement fuses the surfaces, and a joint without it can hold for a few weeks and then weep. A third is pressurizing too soon, since cure time is longer for larger pipe and cold weather.

When PVC Leaks Point to a Bigger Problem

A PVC drain that leaks at several joints can mean the pipe has shifted, the support straps have failed or the house is settling. A leak that returns after a repair may be a pipe that is stressed by movement or a joint that was never properly bonded.

  • Recurring leaks at the same joint mean the root cause was not fixed.
  • Cracks along the pipe wall suggest sun damage, impact or freeze.
  • A wet spot that appears long after use suggests a slow, hidden leak.
  • Leaks at transitions between plastic and metal need the right adapter.

If any of these sound familiar, a plumber can trace the leak and tell you whether a repair or a bigger fix is the better choice.

Safety and Tools for a PVC Repair

Work in a ventilated space because primer and cement give off strong fumes, and keep both away from open flames. Wear gloves and eye protection, and read the cure times on the can. Basic tools are a pipe cutter, a deburring tool, a tape measure and a marker.

Dry-fit every piece before you glue, and mark the pipe so you can see that each joint is fully seated. Glue goes on fast, and you only get a few seconds to push and hold the parts together.

If you are not comfortable with the repair, or the pipe is part of a pressurized supply line, a plumber is the safer choice.

Key Takeaways

  • Dry, prime and cement every PVC joint, and respect the cure time.
  • Do not use PVC cement on CPVC or the other way around.
  • Drain and protect exposed plastic lines before a freeze.
  • Call a plumber for a pressurized leak or one inside a wall.

Questions About PVC Leaks in Camas

Can PVC freeze and crack in Camas winters?

Yes. Water inside a plastic pipe expands when it freezes and can split the pipe, especially in exposed runs. Drain irrigation lines and insulate what you can.

Is PVC safe for drinking water?

PVC pipe that is rated for potable water is used in some cold water applications. Check the markings on the pipe, and use a plumber if you are unsure.

How long should PVC cement cure?

Follow the can. Cure time depends on pipe size, temperature and pressure, and pressurizing too early causes failure.

What if a PVC leak is behind a wall?

Shut off the water and call a plumber. Opening a wall without locating the leak makes the repair bigger.
