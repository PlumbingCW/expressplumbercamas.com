Plumbing for an Outdoor Kitchen: A Camas, WA Homeowner Guide

An outdoor kitchen needs water, a drain and a plan for winter. Here is what to settle before the first trench is dug.

By the Camas Express Plumbing team · Updated November 14, 2026

What an Outdoor Kitchen Needs From Your Plumbing

An outdoor kitchen can be as simple as a sink on a counter or as complete as a full cooking area with a gas line, a refrigerator and a prep sink. Each piece asks something different from your plumbing.

  • Cold water supply for a sink or a bar tap.
  • Hot water if you want it, from a small point-of-use heater or a line from the house.
  • A drain that leads to the right place, not a hose to the lawn.
  • Gas for a grill or cooktop, which needs a licensed gas fitter and a permit in most places.
  • Shutoffs and drains for winter.

Start with the simplest version of the kitchen and add parts you will actually use, since each added line adds trenching, fittings and permits.

Water Supply and Where the Drain Goes

The supply line can tee off an existing outdoor spigot or run from a dedicated branch. A branch with its own shutoff valve inside the house lets you isolate it all winter. The drain is the harder part.

A sink drain generally has to connect to the home's sanitary system or to an approved disposal path, and wastewater should not run onto the ground or into a storm drain. Ask the building department what is allowed, and plan the drain slope before the slab or the pavers go in.

Protecting an Outdoor Kitchen From Freezing

Pacific Northwest winters are mild most of the time and sharp now and then, and one hard night is enough to split a line. Put a shutoff and a low drain on the supply, slope the pipe toward the drain valve and use insulated or buried lines below the frost depth the local code specifies.

Before the first freeze, close the shutoff, open the drain and the faucets and blow out or drain the lines. A winter plan is part of the design, not an afterthought, and if a line does fail, burst pipe repair is the call to make.

Camas Rules for Digging, Backflow and Permits

Before you trench for any outdoor line, Washington requires a call to 811 so utilities can mark buried lines, and utilities must respond at least two business days ahead under RCW 19.122. Plan the dig date around that lead time.

The City of Camas requires underground irrigation and high-hazard connections to have a backflow assembly tested annually by a Certified Backflow Assembly Tester. If your outdoor kitchen ties into an irrigation line, ask the city what applies. For the plumbing and gas permits, check with the Camas Building Division, which enforces the state-adopted plumbing code.

Outdoor connections need backflow protection under the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), commonly a vacuum breaker on a hose bibb, and gas piping has its own code and permit.

Choosing Fixtures and Materials

Use fixtures rated for outdoor use and stainless or brass fittings that tolerate moisture. PEX tubing handles temperature swings and slight freezing better than rigid pipe, and a plumber can help match materials to the design. Keep the shutoff in an accessible spot, not behind a built-in cabinet you cannot open.

If you are planning other work at the same time, such as a new bathroom or a water heater, bundle it. Our water heater installation page covers how that fits.

Hot Water, Appliances and Gas

A small electric point-of-use heater under the sink can supply hot water without a long pipe run, though it needs a power circuit. A line from the house gives hotter water sooner only if it is short and insulated. Many outdoor kitchens do fine with cold water and a pot of hot water from the stove.

Gas appliances need a gas line installed to code, with a shutoff and a pressure test. This is licensed work in most places, and a mistake can be dangerous, so leave it to a qualified gas fitter and get the permit.

Drainage and Sanitation Questions to Ask

  • Where is the nearest sanitary connection, and can the sink reach it with proper slope?
  • Do the rules allow a grease trap or interceptor for a cooking area?
  • Will the sink waste water be allowed to drain to a landscaped area, or must it go to the sewer?
  • Is there a vacuum breaker on any hose connection?

A short conversation with the building department up front prevents tearing out finished work later.

Budgeting and Phasing the Project

Plumbing is often the hidden cost in an outdoor kitchen. Trenching, a permit, a drain connection and a gas line can cost as much as the cabinets, so ask for the plumbing quote early.

If the budget is tight, phase the project. Put in the sleeves and the trench now and add the sink and the gas appliances later. That costs little today and keeps the future options open without tearing up the patio.

Whatever the scope, keep the plans, the permit and the pressure test records in a folder. They help with insurance, resale and future repairs.

Key Takeaways

  • Plan a shutoff and a drain for winter from the start.
  • Ask the building department where an outdoor sink may drain.
  • Call 811 before you dig, and allow at least two business days.
  • Use a licensed fitter and a permit for gas lines.

Questions About Outdoor Kitchen Plumbing in Camas

Do I need a permit for an outdoor kitchen in Camas?

Plumbing and gas work usually needs a permit. Ask the Camas Building Division what applies to your project.

Can an outdoor sink drain to the yard?

Generally no. Drains should connect to an approved system, so ask the building department before you plan the layout.

How do I winterize an outdoor kitchen?

Close the shutoff, open the drain and faucets, and drain or blow out the lines before the first freeze.

Who must test a backflow assembly in Camas?

A Certified Backflow Assembly Tester, annually, for underground irrigation and high-hazard connections under the city's rules.
