Toilet Installation in Camas, WA: What to Know Before You Start

Swapping a toilet looks simple until the flange is damaged or the rough-in does not match. Here is what to check before you lift the old one.

By the Camas Express Plumbing team · Updated November 14, 2026

Is Toilet Installation a Do-It-Yourself Job?

Many homeowners can swap a toilet, and plenty do it successfully. The job goes wrong when the flange is cracked, the floor is soft or the new toilet does not fit the rough-in, and those surprises are easier to handle with a plumber on site.

A toilet weighs a lot and the porcelain cracks if it is dropped or bolts are overtightened. If you are uneasy lifting a heavy fixture, call a plumber and keep your back.

Measure the Rough-In Before You Buy

The rough-in is the distance from the wall behind the toilet to the center of the drain flange. The most common rough-in is twelve inches, but ten and fourteen are also found, and a toilet built for the wrong one will not sit right.

  • Measure from the finished wall, not the baseboard, to the center of the floor bolts.
  • Check the toilet's listed rough-in against your measurement.
  • Look at the shape of the bowl. Round and elongated bowls need different clearances.
  • Check the height. Some people prefer a taller comfort-height bowl.
  • Make sure the new tank fits under any shelf or window sill.

The Flange Decides Everything

The flange is the ring on the floor that the toilet bolts to. It should sit flat on top of the finished floor, be free of cracks and have slots that hold the bolts firmly. A broken or sunken flange makes a toilet rock and eventually leak.

If the flange is damaged, it has to be repaired or replaced before the new toilet goes on, which is a plumber's job when the floor needs work. Our toilet repair page covers rocking toilets and leaks at the base.

Step by Step: What the Installation Involves

  1. Shut off the supply valve, flush and drain the tank and bowl.
  2. Disconnect the supply line and remove the old toilet's bolts.
  3. Lift the toilet, set it aside and scrape away the old wax ring.
  4. Check the flange and floor, then set the new wax ring or seal.
  5. Lower the toilet straight down onto the flange and press it evenly.
  6. Tighten the bolts a little at a time, connect the supply and test for leaks.

If you use a modern seal in place of wax, follow the instructions that come with it.

Water and Sewer Notes for Camas Toilet Installations

Some Camas properties connect to the City of Camas STEP sewer system, where the city owns the tank, pump and control panel, and the property owner maintains the line from the house to the tank. The city says flushable wipes are not suitable for these systems, so any new toilet is best paired with a no-wipes habit.

For new plumbing work, the City of Camas Building Division enforces the state-adopted plumbing code, and a like-for-like toilet swap does not usually raise a code question. If your project moves the toilet or changes the drain, ask the Building Division whether a permit is needed.

Toilet installation clearances and flange requirements are covered by the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), which is one more reason a damaged flange should be repaired and not ignored.

When to Call a Plumber Instead

Call a plumber if the flange is damaged, the floor around it is soft, the drain smells or the toilet clogs even after the install. Those usually signal a deeper problem, and a fresh toilet will not fix them. For a clog that persists, see drain cleaning.

Supply Lines, Valves and Small Parts

A new toilet is the best time to replace the supply line and the stop valve behind it. An old valve that has not been turned in years may leak when you close it, and a braided line that has aged can burst. Replacing both costs little and removes two common failure points.

Pick a supply line long enough to reach without strain and avoid bending it sharply. Hand tighten the nuts and then give them a quarter turn with a wrench, since overtightening can crack the fitting.

After the Toilet Is Set

Run the toilet several times and look at the base and the supply connection for moisture. Check that the toilet sits firmly with no rocking, and set the water level in the tank to the line marked on the inside. If the toilet runs on or the flush is weak, adjust the fill valve and the flapper chain before you cap the bolts.

Finish with a thin bead of caulk around the base, leaving a gap at the back so a future leak can be seen. A toilet that is level, tight and quiet is a good installation, and one that clogs or leaks is a sign to call a plumber.

Tools and Materials for a Toilet Swap

  • A new wax ring or seal, closet bolts and washers.
  • An adjustable wrench, a putty knife and a small level.
  • A new supply line and, if needed, a new stop valve.
  • Towels, a bucket and a sponge for the leftover water.
  • Plastic shims for an uneven floor and a tube of bathroom caulk.

Gather everything before you shut the water off, because an open drain smells and a missing part turns a quick job into a long one. Cover the open flange with a rag while you work to keep sewer gas out of the room.

Finally, plan for disposal. An old toilet is heavy and awkward, and many waste services have rules for porcelain, so ask before you set it on the curb.

Questions About Toilet Installation in Camas

Do I need a permit to replace a toilet in Camas?

A like-for-like swap normally does not, but moving or adding fixtures can. Ask the Camas Building Division if you are unsure.

What kind of toilet should I choose?

Check the rough-in first, then pick the bowl shape and height for the people who use it. Look for models labeled as water-saving.

How long does a toilet installation take?

A straightforward swap is often an hour or two, and a damaged flange or floor adds time.

Can wipes labeled flushable go down a new toilet?

The City of Camas says they are not suitable for its sewer systems, and they cause clogs in any line. Put them in the trash.
