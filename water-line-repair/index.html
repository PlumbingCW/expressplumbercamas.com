Water Line Leak? Shut Off and Check the Meter

Close the shutoff valve at the house and look at the meter. If the dial still moves, water is leaking somewhere between the meter and the house, usually in the service line.

If water is surfacing in the yard, keep people and pets away from soft ground. Call us and we will help you determine whether a shutoff at the meter is needed, and ask the City water division where its responsibility ends at your meter.

Repair or Replace the Service Line

A spot repair makes sense when one fitting or one short section has failed and the rest of the line is in good shape. When the pipe is old, brittle or has leaked repeatedly, replacing the full line is better.

  • Repair a single break with a coupling or a new section.
  • Replace the line from meter to house when the material is failing.
  • Install new shutoff valves so you can control the water next time.
  • Add a pressure regulator if high pressure is a stress on the line.
  • Restore the yard after the dig.

If you are not sure where the water is going, our leak detection page explains how we trace it, and if the failure is inside the home, burst pipe repair applies.

Water Lines, 811 and the City Meter in Camas

The City of Camas Public Works Water Division serves roughly 23,500 consumers, and the water system includes over 162 miles of pipeline. Your service line connects your property to that system at the meter, so ask the division where its responsibility ends and yours begins.

Washington law requires an 811 call before digging, and utilities must mark buried lines at least two business days ahead under RCW 19.122. Plan for that, and ask the Camas Building Division whether your replacement needs a permit.

Water service lines must use approved pipe under the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted in Washington, and the local water utility sets its own rules at the meter.

What to Expect When We Dig

We locate the line, call 811 for utilities and mark the dig area with you. Digging by hand near other lines and utilities keeps them safe, and we protect your landscaping where we can by laying the soil on tarps.

After the repair, we pressure test the line, backfill and tamp the soil. We tell you how the yard will settle and what to watch for in the weeks that follow.

Protecting Your New Water Line

After a repair, keep heavy equipment off the trench for a few weeks while the soil settles, and avoid planting deep-rooted trees directly over the line. Add a shutoff valve at the house if you lack one, and know where the meter and its valves are.

If you are replacing the whole line, ask about a pressure regulator and a new valve at the entry point, which protect the new pipe and make future repairs easier.

Homes on larger lots often have a longer run from meter to house, and our Prune Hill page explains what that means for hillside properties.

Do you handle water line emergencies at night or on weekends in Camas?

Yes. A leaking line does not wait for Monday, and the 24/7 line at (360) 371-6092 is open.

How do I know my service line is leaking?

Common signs are a wet or sunken patch in the yard, a meter that moves with everything off and low pressure.

Can you replace the line without digging a trench?

Sometimes other methods fit. We look at the site and explain what is possible before work starts.

Who pays if the leak is before the meter?

The meter is generally the dividing point, but ask the City of Camas water division for your exact situation.
