Disposal Jammed or Leaking? Safe First Steps

Switch off the disposal at the wall and, if you can, at the breaker. Never put your hand into a disposal, even when it is off, because a stuck blade can move when freed.

Shine a flashlight in the opening and use tongs for anything visible. If the unit hums, press the red reset button on the bottom of the unit, then try again after a few minutes.

Common Disposal Problems

Hums but will not spin

A jam, often a bone, a fruit pit or a spoon, holds the flywheel.

Leaks under the sink

A cracked housing, a failed seal or a loose drain connection.

Slow drain with the disposal

A clog in the sink line past the disposal.

Bad smell

Food residue in the grinding chamber or the splash guard.

A jam is usually a short visit, while a leaking housing means replacement because the unit cannot be resealed. If the clog is in the sink line past the unit, our drain cleaning service clears it.

Disposals and Camas Sewer Connections

Whether a property uses a gravity sewer or the City of Camas STEP system is worth knowing before you grind a lot of food waste. The city owns the tank, the pump and the control panel in a STEP system, and asks owners to avoid additives and flushable wipes, so ask the city what it recommends for kitchen waste at your address.

The most reliable habits for any kitchen line are the same. Run cold water while the unit is on, skip grease, fibrous scraps and coffee grounds, and keep a plunger and a flashlight nearby.

The Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) sets how a disposal and a dishwasher connect to the drain, including how the dishwasher drain is protected from backflow, so those connections are checked during a repair.

How to Make a Disposal Last Longer

  • Run cold water before, during and for a few seconds after grinding.
  • Feed scraps in small amounts rather than all at once.
  • Avoid grease, fibrous scraps, coffee grounds, pasta, rice and bones.
  • Clean the splash guard and the chamber with ice and citrus peel now and then.
  • Never pour a chemical drain opener into a disposal.

Most disposals last for years with a little care, and the ones that fail early usually fail from jams, leaks or a clog past the unit that was never cleared.

When Replacement Makes More Sense

A disposal that has been repaired more than once, leaks from the housing or is more than ten years old is usually better replaced. A new unit gives you a fresh warranty from the maker and quieter operation, and we install it with new mounting hardware and a clean drain connection.

We also look at the sink and the drain line during the visit, since a failing disposal is often the first sign of a clog or a leak elsewhere under the sink.
