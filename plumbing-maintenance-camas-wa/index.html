Plumbing Maintenance and Repairs in Camas, WA: A Seasonal Checklist

Most plumbing emergencies give a warning first. This seasonal checklist helps a Camas homeowner catch the warnings and fix small things before they grow.

By the Camas Express Plumbing team · Updated November 14, 2026

Why Maintenance Beats Emergency Repair

A slow drip, a gurgling drain or a water heater that rumbles is cheap to deal with early and expensive to ignore. Plumbing problems rarely fix themselves, and most grow in the dark behind walls and under floors.

A few simple checks each season keep a home out of the emergency column. None of them need special tools, and several take only a few minutes.

Spring: Check After the Wet Season

  • Look under every sink for moisture, stains or swollen cabinet floors.
  • Run every drain and note any that are slow or gurgling.
  • Check hose bibs for drips and inspect outdoor lines damaged by winter.
  • Test the main shutoff valve to confirm it turns, then reopen it.
  • Look at the base of the water heater for rust or moisture.

Summer: Watch the Outdoors and the Meter

Irrigation season uses more water, so a leak in a sprinkler line can hide behind a high bill. Read your water meter before and after a two-hour period when no one is using water, and if the dial moves, you have a leak. Summer is also a good time to flush the water heater, since you will not miss hot water if the job takes a while.

Fall: Prepare for Freezing Weather

  • Disconnect and drain garden hoses, and cover or insulate hose bibs.
  • Insulate exposed pipes in crawlspaces, garages and exterior walls.
  • Learn where the main shutoff is and make sure everyone in the house knows.
  • Drain and shut down irrigation and outdoor kitchen lines.
  • Check for gaps in the foundation where cold air reaches pipes.

If you find a pipe that has frozen or burst, call quickly. Our burst pipe repair page covers the steps, and the 24/7 line at (360) 371-6092 is open.

Winter: Respond to Cold Snaps

On a freezing night, open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls, let a faucet drip slightly and keep the heat on if you leave town. A trickle of water costs little compared with a burst pipe.

Repairs Worth Scheduling Before They Fail

Some repairs are worth doing on a schedule rather than waiting for failure. A water heater past ten years is a candidate for planned replacement, a toilet that runs is a quick fix and a main line with recurring slow drains deserves a camera look. Replacing the old gate valve at your main shutoff can pay for itself the first time you need it.

Other items include replacing braided supply lines every several years, since rubber and braid age out, and checking the pressure relief valve on the water heater. If your water pressure is high, a regulator protects every fixture in the house.

Maintenance Tips Specific to Camas

The City of Camas explains that its meters include a leak indicator, where a blinking faucet icon means water has flowed at least half the day, every day, and a steady icon means flow all day. If you see the icon, check toilets, irrigation and the water heater before calling the city, and wait 24 hours after a repair before judging the meter.

If your property is on the city's STEP sewer system, keep the two green tank lids uncovered with at least 1 inch of clearance, watch the outside alarm panel and avoid additives and flushable wipes. The city pumps the tank every six to ten years, and the line from your house to the tank is the owner's to maintain.

The Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) requires a pressure relief valve on a water heater for a reason, so testing it and replacing a weeping one is a standard maintenance item.

Signs You Should Call a Plumber Now

Call right away for sewage backing up, a gas smell, water spraying from a pipe, no water at all, or a water heater leaking from the tank body. For slow drains, a running toilet or a dripping faucet, schedule a visit soon, since these usually get worse.

A Simple Monthly Habit

Pick one day a month and do three things. Look under the kitchen and bathroom sinks for moisture, run water in rarely used drains such as a basement floor drain or a guest bath so the traps stay wet, and glance at your water heater for drips or rust.

It takes ten minutes and catches most slow problems. Write down what you notice so that when something changes you have a baseline, and call a plumber when the notes show a trend.

Keep a Plumbing Record

  • Date and brand of your water heater and its rating label.
  • Location of the main shutoff and fixture shutoffs.
  • Past repairs, who did them and the receipts.
  • Meter readings from before and after any suspected leak.
  • Photos of your pipes in crawlspaces or basements for future reference.

When to Schedule a Professional Checkup

A yearly plumbing checkup is worth considering for older homes and for anyone who has had a past leak. A plumber looks at the water heater, the shutoffs, the pressure, the hose bibs, the drains and the accessible pipes, and gives you a list in order of urgency.

You can pair the visit with a camera look at the main line every few years if you have large trees near the sewer. The goal is a short list of small fixes you choose on your own schedule, instead of one big one that picks you.

Ask for the list in writing, and keep it in your plumbing record. It gives the next plumber a head start.

Questions About Plumbing Maintenance in Camas

What should a Camas homeowner do with the STEP sewer tank lids?

Keep the two green lids uncovered with at least 1 inch of clearance, as the City of Camas asks, and keep the alarm panel visible.

How can I tell if I have a hidden leak?

Shut off all water use, read the meter, wait 15 minutes and read it again. If the number moved, call for leak detection.

How often should I flush my water heater?

Many manufacturers suggest yearly. Follow the label for your unit.

Should I replace old supply hoses?

Braided hoses and rubber connectors age, so replacing them every several years is cheap insurance.
