What Is a Hybrid Water Heater? A Camas, WA Homeowner Guide

A hybrid water heater is an electric tank with a heat pump on top. Here is how it works, where it fits and what it means for a Camas home.

By the Camas Express Plumbing team · Updated November 14, 2026

What a Hybrid Water Heater Actually Is

A hybrid water heater, also called a heat pump water heater, is a storage tank that heats water with a heat pump instead of relying only on electric heating elements. The heat pump pulls warmth from the air around the unit and moves it into the water, which takes less electricity than making the same heat with a resistance element.

The tank still has backup elements, so you get hot water even when the demand is high or the room is cold. Most units let you choose a mode, from an efficiency setting that leans on the heat pump to a faster setting that uses the elements too.

How It Works Day to Day

The heat pump sits in a housing above the tank, with a fan and a coil. It draws in room air, takes heat out of it, and sends cooler, drier air back out. That is why placement matters, because the unit needs a room with enough open air around it to keep working at full efficiency.

  • Air in, cooler air out. The room where the unit sits will run a little cooler.
  • Condensate. Pulling moisture from the air makes water that has to drain somewhere.
  • Noise. A fan and compressor run, about as loud as a dehumidifier.
  • Recovery. In efficiency mode, recovery is slower than a standard electric tank.

If your household uses lots of hot water at once, a larger tank or a mode that uses the elements keeps up with demand.

Where a Hybrid Water Heater Fits in a Camas Home

Garages, basements and large utility rooms are good places for a hybrid unit. A closet with a door, on the other hand, may starve the unit of air unless it has a louvered door or ducting. Mild Pacific Northwest garages suit heat pump units better than very cold buildings, because the heat pump works best when the surrounding air is not freezing.

Costs and Trade-Offs to Weigh

A hybrid unit costs more than a standard electric tank, and the saving comes through lower electricity use over its life. How quickly that pays back depends on your electric rate, how much hot water you use and any rebate you qualify for.

Other trade-offs include the space the unit takes, the sound and the need for a condensate drain. A plumber can look at the room and tell you whether a hybrid fits before you buy one.

Sizing a Hybrid Water Heater

Size by the number of people and the peak use in the house. A common starting point is a tank in the 50 to 80 gallon range, but a family with several showers a day may want the larger size, and a couple may do fine with less.

Clark Public Utilities and Heat Pump Water Heaters

Clark Public Utilities runs a heat pump water heater program for Clark County homes. The rebate applies when you replace an electric resistance storage water heater with a heat pump model that meets Tier 3 or Tier 4 of the Northern Climate Specification, and the utility sets the rules and the amounts.

Because the amounts and eligibility change, check the utility's current program page before you buy, and keep the model number and the receipt. If you plan to apply, tell your plumber so the unit you pick is on the qualifying list. We do not quote rebate amounts here for that reason.

Water heater installation standards in Washington follow the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and heat pump models add the maker's instructions for airflow and condensate drainage.

Maintenance for a Hybrid Water Heater

Hybrid units need an air filter cleaned a few times a year and a condensate drain that stays clear. Like any tank, they also benefit from a periodic flush and an anode rod check. A neglected filter or clogged drain is the most common reason a hybrid unit underperforms.

Hybrid Water Heater Modes and Settings

Most hybrid units offer several modes. An efficiency mode runs only the heat pump, a hybrid mode adds the elements when demand is high, a high demand mode leans on the elements for speed and a vacation mode idles the tank. Learning which mode suits your household is half the value of the unit.

In a mild garage the heat pump can carry most of the load most of the year. In a very cold space the unit shifts to the elements automatically, which raises the electric use, so keep that in mind when you pick the location.

Is a Hybrid Water Heater Right for Your House?

Ask three questions. Do you have an electric tank today, a spot with open air around it and a way to drain condensate? If the answer to all three is yes, a hybrid unit is a strong candidate. If you heat with gas, the math changes, and a gas tank or a tankless unit may suit better.

Also think about timing. A hybrid unit costs more up front, so if you are replacing a failed tank in a hurry, a standard swap may be the practical call today, with a hybrid on the list for the next replacement.

Questions About Hybrid Water Heaters in Camas

Does Clark Public Utilities offer a rebate for a hybrid water heater in Camas?

Clark Public Utilities has a heat pump water heater program for Clark County homes replacing an electric resistance storage water heater with a qualifying Tier 3 or Tier 4 unit. Confirm the current amount and rules with the utility before buying.

Will a hybrid water heater make my garage cold?

It cools the air near the unit slightly, and in a mild garage the effect is small. In a very small closed room it can be noticeable, so airflow matters.

Can I put a hybrid water heater where my old tank is?

Often, but it is taller than a standard tank and needs air around it and a place for condensate to drain. A plumber should look at the space.

Is a hybrid water heater loud?

It makes a fan and compressor sound similar to a dehumidifier, so avoid placing it next to a bedroom wall.
