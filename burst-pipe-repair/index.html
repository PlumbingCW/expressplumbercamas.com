Pipe Burst? Do These Five Things Right Now

  1. Close the main water shutoff valve, usually near the meter, where the line enters the house, or at the water heater.
  2. Switch off power to any wet area from the breaker panel if you can reach it safely.
  3. Open the lowest faucet in the house to drain the lines.
  4. Move valuables, rugs and electronics out of the water's path.
  5. Photograph the damage for your records, then call us.

If you cannot find the shutoff, call anyway and we will help you locate it by phone. Knowing the shutoff location before an emergency saves the most damage.

How We Repair a Burst Pipe

Once the water is off, we find the failed section, cut out the damaged pipe and replace it with the right material and fittings. We pressure test the repair before the water goes back on.

If the failure came from a hard freeze, we look at the rest of the system for other weak spots, because pipes that freeze once often have more than one split. We also advise on drying out and who to call for restoration, and we point you to leak detection if water is still unaccounted for.

Camas and the January 2024 Freeze

The Camas-Washougal Post-Record reported that during the January 2024 winter storm, frozen pipes burst at Skyridge Middle School on Sunday, January 14, releasing about 3,200 gallons of water across 22 classrooms and the library. School administrators believed the pipes at the 28-year-old building were not adequately insulated, and Prune Hill Elementary also had frozen-pipe leaks in its gym and cafeteria, with at least $250,000 in damage reported.

The lesson for a home is plain. Insulate exposed pipes, know where your main shutoff is, and let faucets drip on a hard freeze night. If you are leaving for a few days in winter, shut off the water and drain the lines or keep the heat on.

Repairs that replace sections of supply line are expected to meet the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as Washington adopts it, including approved materials and fittings.

After the Water Is Off: Cleanup and Insurance

Once the leak is stopped, remove standing water and open up wet areas to dry. Wet drywall and insulation hold moisture for days, so run fans and dehumidifiers and take photos of everything before you start. Keep damaged items until your insurer has seen them.

We repair the pipe, but drying, drywall and flooring are separate trades. Ask your insurer which restoration companies they work with, and keep the written details of our repair for the claim.

Preventing the Next Burst Pipe

Insulate pipes in unheated areas, seal drafts around pipes in exterior walls and know where your main shutoff is. On freezing nights, open cabinet doors and let a faucet drip, and if you travel in winter, keep the heat on or drain the system.

If a pipe has burst once, ask us to check the rest of the system for weak spots. Corroded or poorly supported pipe is often the first to fail.

What should I do if a frozen pipe has not burst yet?

Keep the heat on, open the faucet slightly and warm the pipe gently with a hair dryer. Never use an open flame.

Will homeowner's insurance cover a burst pipe?

Coverage varies by policy. Document the damage with photos and call your insurer, and keep our written repair details for the claim.

How can I prevent pipes from freezing?

Insulate exposed pipes, seal gaps in exterior walls and know your shutoff. Disconnect hoses before freezing nights.
