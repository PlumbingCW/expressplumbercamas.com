How to Install a Toilet Wax Ring With the Flange: A Camas, WA Guide

The wax ring is a small part that keeps sewer water and gas in the pipe. Here is how it works with the flange and how to set one right.

By the Camas Express Plumbing team · Updated November 14, 2026

What the Wax Ring Does

A wax ring is a thick gasket that sits between the base of the toilet and the flange. It forms a seal so that water and sewer gas go down the drain and not onto the floor. When it fails, you may see water at the base, smell sewage or feel the toilet rock.

The ring is made to be used once. If you lift the toilet for any reason, the ring has to be replaced, because the wax will not reseal the same way after it has been compressed.

Check the Flange Before You Set the Ring

The flange is the ring bolted to the floor around the drain opening. It should be solid, level and sit on top of the finished floor, not below it. Slots on the flange hold the closet bolts that anchor the toilet.

  • Cracked or broken ring. A cracked flange cannot hold bolts, so the toilet will rock.
  • Flange below the floor. A flange that sits lower than the finished floor needs a spacer or extension.
  • Rusted flange. Corroded metal flanges may need replacing.
  • Loose flange. A flange that moves must be re-secured to the subfloor.
  • Soft floor. A wet or soft subfloor must be repaired before a toilet goes back on.

If the flange is damaged, a repair ring or replacement flange is the fix. The toilet repair page explains when a plumber handles it.

Setting a Wax Ring: Step by Step

  1. Shut off the supply and flush the toilet to empty it.
  2. Disconnect the supply line and unscrew the nuts from the closet bolts.
  3. Lift the toilet straight up and set it on a towel or cardboard.
  4. Scrape the old wax from the flange and check the flange for damage.
  5. Place the new wax ring on the flange or on the bottom of the toilet, following the manufacturer's directions.
  6. Lower the toilet straight down onto the bolts, press down evenly and tighten the nuts a little at a time.
  7. Reconnect the supply line, open the valve and test for leaks.

Do not rock the toilet side to side once it is down, since that can break the seal. If you need to adjust it, lift it and reset the ring.

Why a Toilet Rocks and Leaks

A toilet that rocks is almost always a sign of a bad seal, loose bolts, an uneven floor or a damaged flange. Tightening bolts harder will crack the porcelain, so do not use more force than needed. Shims under the base can level a toilet on an uneven floor, and a bead of caulk around the base finishes the job, leaving a gap at the back so leaks can show.

Camas Floors, Water and Sewer Connections

Many Camas homes sit on older slabs and wood floors that have seen decades of bathroom moisture. A slow leak at a toilet base can soften the subfloor, so if the floor near your toilet feels spongy, the plumber may need to repair it before resetting the toilet.

On the City of Camas STEP sewer system, the owner maintains the line from the house to the tank, while the city owns the tank, pump and control panel. The city asks that flushable wipes not be used, so any toilet repair that follows repeated clogs is a good time to review what goes down the toilet.

The Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) covers how a toilet connects to the drain, including a flange secured to the floor, which is the standard a repair should return the toilet to.

Special Cases: Tile, Uneven Floors and Old Flanges

Remodels often raise the floor with new tile, which leaves the flange below the surface. A flange extender or a thicker seal can bridge a small gap, but a large gap needs a proper spacer or a new flange set at the right height.

An uneven floor makes a toilet rock even with a good seal. Plastic shims can level the base, and a thin bead of caulk covers the edge, leaving the back open. If the floor is badly out of level, flooring work may be needed.

Signs the Seal Has Failed Again

  • Water or staining around the base.
  • A sewage smell in the bathroom.
  • The toilet moves when you sit down.
  • A soft or discolored floor near the toilet.
  • A ceiling stain in the room below.

If any of these return after a new ring, the problem is probably the flange or the floor, and a plumber should take a look.

Tools and Materials for Setting a Wax Ring

  • A new wax ring or seal, sized for the toilet.
  • New closet bolts and washers.
  • A putty knife and rags for cleaning the flange.
  • A level and plastic shims.
  • A bucket and a sponge for leftover water.

Warm wax is easier to work with and seals better, so do not set it in a cold garage. Keep the ring in the house for an hour before use, and handle it by the edges so it keeps its shape.

Set the toilet once and press it straight down. If you must lift it again, start over with a new ring, and take that as a reminder that a good seal is a one-shot job.

Key Takeaways

  • A wax ring seals once, so replace it any time the toilet is lifted.
  • A damaged flange makes a toilet rock and leak.
  • Tighten bolts a little at a time to avoid cracking the porcelain.
  • A soft floor near a toilet needs repair before the toilet goes back.

Questions About Wax Rings and Flanges in Camas

Can I reuse a wax ring?

No. Once compressed, it will not reseal properly, so use a new one each time the toilet is lifted.

How do I know my flange is damaged?

Rocking, rust, cracks or a flange that sits below the floor are the usual signs.

Is a soft floor around a toilet an emergency?

It is a sign of long-term moisture, so schedule a repair soon, and shut off the toilet if it is actively leaking.

Do wipes matter for the Camas sewer?

The City of Camas says flushable wipes are not suitable for its sewer systems, and they cause clogs in many lines.
