Emergency Water Heater Replacement in Camas, WA

A water heater that has failed leaves you without hot water and sometimes with a flooded utility room. Call (360) 371-6092 and we will help you choose a replacement that fits your home and your fixtures.

Tank Failed? Stop the Water Before Anything Else

If the tank is leaking, close the cold water supply valve on the pipe above the heater and turn off power at the breaker or the gas control. A tank that has split cannot be repaired, so the goal is to limit the water on the floor.

Move anything stored near the heater, and open a hot faucet somewhere upstairs to relieve pressure if the tank is still draining. Then call, and we will walk through what size and type to look at.

How We Choose the Right Replacement

Replacement starts with how your household uses hot water, not with the biggest tank on the shelf. A family with two bathrooms and a dishwasher needs something different from a couple in a small ranch.

  • Capacity or flow rate matched to the number of people and fixtures running at once.
  • Fuel type based on what is already in the house, with electric and gas the common pair in Clark County.
  • Venting and clearances checked against the current plumbing code.
  • Expansion tank and relief valve upgraded when the old setup falls short.
  • Heat pump models for homes that qualify for local utility programs.

If you want to compare a new tank with an on-demand model, our tankless water heater page lays out the trade-offs, and our water heater installation page covers planned upgrades.

Permits, Code and Replacement in Camas

Washington adopts the Uniform Plumbing Code with state amendments through WAC 51-56, and the City of Camas Building Division enforces the 2021 codes that took effect on March 15, 2024. A water heater swap can involve a permit depending on the scope, so we ask the Building Division what applies and tell you before work starts.

Clark Public Utilities also offers a rebate for replacing an electric resistance storage water heater with a qualifying heat pump model. Because the details change, confirm eligibility with the utility before you commit to a unit.

A replacement unit has to meet the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted in Washington through WAC 51-56, including relief valve discharge and venting rules, and we confirm both before the old tank leaves.

What Happens on Replacement Day

We shut off the water and the power or gas, drain the old tank and disconnect it. The new unit goes in with fresh connections, a proper relief valve and any code updates we discussed. We fill, purge, test and light it, and then we show you the controls.

Expect a few hours for a standard swap. Longer jobs include fuel changes, relocations and tankless conversions, and we give you a realistic window before we start.

Replacement Questions Answered

Do you handle water heater replacement emergencies at night or on weekends in Camas?

Yes. A failed tank at 2 a.m. gets the same line as everything else, open 24/7 at (360) 371-6092. We tell you what is possible that day and what is not.

How do I know it is time to replace instead of repair?

A leak from the tank body, a unit well past ten years old, or repeated repairs in a short window all point to replacement. A younger tank with a bad element is usually worth repairing.

Should I switch to tankless?

It depends on your fixtures, your gas or electric service and your budget. We size it against how many outlets run at once, and we tell you if a tank is the better fit.

What happens to my old water heater?

We remove it and arrange proper disposal, so you are not left with a dead tank in the garage.
