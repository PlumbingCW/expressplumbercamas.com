Plumbing a New House in Camas, WA: What to Plan For

New construction is the best time to get plumbing right, because nothing is behind a wall yet. Here is what to decide, and when.

By the Camas Express Plumbing team · Updated November 14, 2026

The Three Phases of New-House Plumbing

Plumbing in a new house happens in stages that match the construction schedule. Missing one stage, or planning it late, is the most common reason owners end up with an awkward layout or a pricey change order.

  1. Underground and slab rough-in. Drain lines, vents and sleeves are placed before concrete is poured.
  2. Rough-in. Supply and drain lines are run through open framing, and the inspector reviews them before the walls close.
  3. Finish. Fixtures, faucets, water heater and appliances are set and tested.

Each phase needs a plumbing inspection by the building department. Plan the schedule with your builder so the plumber is on site before the insulation and drywall crews.

Layout Decisions Worth Settling Early

  • Fixture locations. Moving a toilet after the slab is poured is expensive.
  • Water heater location. Keep it near the busiest fixtures and leave service access.
  • Main shutoff. Put it where it is easy to reach, and add shutoffs at each fixture.
  • Hose bibs. Choose freeze-proof types and place them with winter in mind.
  • Future needs. Stub out a line for a future bathroom, wet bar or laundry sink.

Think about the whole system as well, including the water heater choice. Our water heater installation page and the guide to tankless units describe the options for new homes.

Material Choices for Supply and Drain Lines

Most new homes use PEX or copper for supply lines and PVC or ABS for drain lines. PEX is flexible and tolerates freezing better than rigid pipe, while copper is rigid and long-established. For drains, plastic is common and quiet, though cast iron is sometimes specified near bedrooms for sound control.

Permits and Inspections

New plumbing work needs a permit, and inspections happen at each stage. Do not cover pipe until it has passed, because the inspector needs to see it. A good plumber coordinates the timing and corrects anything flagged on the spot.

Camas Codes, Permits and Utility Locates for New Homes

The City of Camas Building Division administers the state-adopted Uniform Plumbing Code, and the 2021 I-Codes took effect in Camas on March 15, 2024. In Washington, the Uniform Plumbing Code is adopted with state amendments through WAC 51-56, and plumbing contractors register with the Department of Labor and Industries under RCW 18.106.

Before any excavation for water or sewer lines, Washington requires a call to 811, and utilities mark buried lines at least two business days in advance under RCW 19.122. If your lot may connect to the City's STEP sewer system, ask the city early about how the connection works, because the city owns the tank, pump and panel and the owner maintains the line from the house to the tank.

At every stage the standard to build to is the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) with Washington amendments, and the inspector measures the work against it.

Backflow and Irrigation

If your landscaping plan includes underground irrigation, the City of Camas requires a backflow assembly that is tested annually by a Certified Backflow Assembly Tester. Plan the location for the assembly with the landscaper, and keep the test record.

Questions to Ask a Plumber Before You Hire

  • Are you registered with the Washington Department of Labor and Industries, and can I verify it?
  • Who pulls the permit and schedules the inspections?
  • What materials are you proposing, and why?
  • How do you protect the lines from freezing in exterior walls?
  • What is your plan for the rough-in and finish schedule?
  • What will you need from the builder, and by when?

A good answer is specific. A vague answer or a quote that leaves out permits and inspections is a signal to ask more questions.

Water Pressure, Shutoffs and Future Service

New homes benefit from a pressure regulator at the main, so every fixture sees a steady level. Add shutoffs at each fixture and a main shutoff that is easy to reach. If you are installing a water heater, leave space for service and a drain pan or a drain path.

Think about what you may want later. A rough-in for a future bathroom, an extra hose bib or a stub for a water softener costs little now and much more after the walls are closed.

A Sample New-Build Plumbing Timeline

  1. Before the slab: plan fixture locations and set drain lines and sleeves.
  2. After framing: run rough-in supply, drain and vent lines.
  3. Inspection: the building department reviews the rough-in before insulation.
  4. After drywall: install fixtures, faucets and the water heater.
  5. Final: pressure test, flush the lines and pass the final inspection.

Keep each step on the builder's schedule. A plumber who arrives late can hold up the drywall crew, and a plumber who is not told about a design change can end up redoing work.

Key Takeaways

  • Settle fixture locations before the slab or walls close.
  • Plan for inspections at each stage, and never cover pipe early.
  • Check your plumber's registration on the L&I tool.
  • Rough in extras now, since adding them later costs far more.

The best new-house plumbing job is the one you never think about again. Good planning, clear communication with your builder and a plumber who explains each step make that possible, and they are well worth the effort before the walls go up.

Questions About Plumbing a New House in Camas

Do I need a permit for new plumbing in Camas?

Yes, new plumbing work typically requires a permit and inspections through the City of Camas Building Division.

What plumbing code does Camas use?

Washington adopts the Uniform Plumbing Code with state amendments, and the 2021 codes took effect in Camas on March 15, 2024.

How can I check a plumbing contractor in Washington?

Use the Labor and Industries Verify a Contractor tool to check registration, bond, workers' comp status and violations.

Should I choose PEX or copper?

Both work. PEX is cheaper and freeze tolerant, and copper has a long track record. Your builder and plumber can talk through the trade-offs.
