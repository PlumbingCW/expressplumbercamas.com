How Hot Does a Tankless Water Heater Get? A Camas, WA Homeowner Guide

Tankless heaters can run hotter than you need. Here is how the temperature works, what to set it to and why the number matters.

By the Camas Express Plumbing team · Updated November 14, 2026

The Short Answer

A tankless water heater heats water to whatever set point you choose, within the range the maker allows. Many residential models can be set well above what a household needs, so the better question is how hot you should set it.

The US Department of Energy suggests 120 degrees Fahrenheit for most households, and many people find that comfortable for showers and dishes. Higher settings increase scald risk and waste energy.

How the Temperature Is Controlled

A tankless unit measures the temperature of the incoming water and the outgoing water and adjusts the burner or the heating element to hit the set point. The ability to do that depends on how fast water flows and how much the water needs to be heated.

If you open too many fixtures at once, the unit may not be able to heat all the water to the set point, and you will feel the temperature drop. That is the flow limit at work, and it is the reason sizing matters, which we cover on our tankless water heater page.

Why Incoming Water Temperature Matters

The cold water coming into your house is colder in winter than in summer. A tankless unit has to raise the temperature of the water by a certain number of degrees, and the colder the water, the harder the job.

  • In winter, incoming water in the Pacific Northwest can be quite cold, so the unit works harder.
  • In summer, the same unit can supply a higher flow at the same set point.
  • Sizing is based on the winter temperature rise, not the summer one.
  • A unit that works great in July may struggle on a January morning.

Scald Risk and Safety

Water at 140 degrees can cause serious scald burns in seconds, and young children and older adults are at the highest risk. If you set a higher temperature for any reason, use thermostatic mixing valves at fixtures so what reaches the tap is safe.

If your tankless unit delivers water that is too hot or fluctuates wildly, call a plumber. It may be a sensor, a scale issue or a flow problem, and our water heater repair service can diagnose it.

Camas Water, Temperature Rise and Tankless Performance

The City of Camas draws its drinking water from groundwater wells and stores it in reservoirs. The city does not publish hardness in its 2025 Consumer Confidence Report, so test your water to learn whether scale will affect your unit's heat exchanger.

Scale coats the heat exchanger and reduces the temperature the unit can deliver. A descaling flush on the manufacturer's schedule keeps performance up, and a plumber can check for error codes if the temperature starts to wander.

Water heater temperature guidance comes from the US Department of Energy, and the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) has its own rules for relief valves and tempering.

Recirculation and Wait Time

Many homeowners notice a delay before hot water arrives, and a tankless unit does not remove it. The delay is the time hot water needs to travel from the unit to the tap, and in a long run the water in the pipe has gone cold.

A recirculation pump and a return line keep a small flow moving so hot water is waiting near the fixtures. It adds some energy use, so some households use a timer or a button to run it only at busy times.

Troubleshooting Temperature Problems

  • Water too cold. Check the set point, the number of fixtures running, scale buildup and any error codes.
  • Water swings hot and cold. A flow sensor, a mixing issue or a low flow at the fixture can confuse the unit.
  • No hot water. Check the power, the gas supply, the venting and the display for a code.
  • Water too hot. Check the set point and the thermostatic valves at the fixture.

If the display shows an error code, write it down before calling. The code points to the cause and saves time during the visit, and our team can help at (360) 371-6092.

Choosing a Set Point for Your Household

A household with young children or older adults leans toward the lower end of the range, while a home that runs a dishwasher without a heater might choose a slightly higher setting. Some households add a mixing valve at the heater so the water in the pipes is hot enough and the water at the tap is safe.

Try a setting for a week and see how it feels. Adjust one step at a time and keep a note of the number, so you can return to it after a service visit that resets the controls.

If the unit has a remote controller or an app, check which temperatures it can reach and whether a child lock is available.

Key Takeaways

  • Choose a set point around the level the Department of Energy suggests, and go higher only with mixing valves.
  • Winter incoming water is colder, so size the unit for January and not July.
  • Too many open fixtures lower the temperature the unit can deliver.
  • Scale reduces performance, so descale on the maker's schedule.
  • Write down any error code before you call for service.

Questions About Tankless Temperature in Camas

What temperature should I set my tankless water heater to?

The US Department of Energy suggests 120 degrees Fahrenheit for most households. Set it higher only with safety measures in place.

Why does my tankless water get cold when two showers run?

The unit has a flow limit, and running too many fixtures at once reduces the temperature rise it can deliver.

Will my tankless unit work as well in January as in July?

Colder incoming water in winter means a bigger temperature rise is needed, so a unit sized for summer may struggle.

Is scale a problem in Camas?

The city does not publish a hardness figure, so test your water at the tap and descale as the manufacturer advises.
