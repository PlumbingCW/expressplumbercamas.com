Main Water Shutoff Valve: How to Find and Use It in Camas, WA

When a pipe bursts, knowing where the main shutoff is limits the damage. Here is how to find yours and make sure it works.

By the Camas Express Plumbing team · Updated November 14, 2026

Why the Main Shutoff Matters

The main shutoff valve stops water at the point where it enters your house. When a pipe bursts, a water heater tank fails or a supply line lets go, closing that valve is the single best thing you can do to limit damage.

Most people find out where it is during an emergency, with water pouring down the wall. A few minutes spent now, finding it and making sure it turns, is worth more than any insurance claim form.

Where to Look for the Shutoff

  • Near the water heater. Many homes have a valve on the cold line close to the tank.
  • In the basement or crawlspace. Look where the main line enters through the foundation.
  • In the garage. A valve often sits on an interior wall next to the street side.
  • In a utility room. Look near the laundry area or a mechanical closet.
  • Outside near the meter. The meter box in the ground often has a valve on the street side and another on the house side.

The valve comes in two main types. A gate valve has a round handle you turn several times, and a ball valve has a lever that turns a quarter turn. A ball valve is more reliable and faster in an emergency, so many plumbers recommend replacing an old gate valve.

How to Test Your Valve

  1. Locate the valve and make sure you can reach it without clutter in the way.
  2. Turn it slowly toward closed. A gate valve turns clockwise, and a ball valve turns until the lever is crossways to the pipe.
  3. Open a faucet in the house to confirm that the water has stopped.
  4. Open the valve again and check for drips around the handle.
  5. Tag the valve so everyone in the household knows where it is.

If the valve is stiff, do not force it with a wrench. A valve that has been left alone for years can break when it is turned, and if it fails you are left with a larger problem. This is a good time to call a plumber and ask about a replacement, which our plumbing installation service can include.

What to Do When a Pipe Has Burst

Close the main shutoff first. Then open the lowest faucet to drain the lines, switch off the water heater if you can reach it, and move valuables from the wet area. Call a plumber, and if water is already near electrical outlets, stay away from them.

Camas Meters, Shutoffs and the January 2024 Freeze

The City of Camas Public Works Water Division serves about 23,500 consumers, and its meters include a leak indicator that blinks when water has flowed for at least half the day. The meter is the utility's equipment, so ask the city before you operate a valve on its side and use your own house shutoff for emergencies.

The Camas-Washougal Post-Record reported that frozen pipes at Skyridge Middle School burst on January 14, 2024, releasing about 3,200 gallons across 22 classrooms and the library. A known shutoff location and a valve that moves are what turn a flood into a mop-up.

The Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) calls for accessible shutoff valves on the building water supply and on individual fixtures, so a stuck valve is a code and a safety concern.

Upgrading an Old Shutoff Valve

A shutoff valve that is stiff, leaking at the handle or sticking halfway is worth replacing before it fails. The job means shutting off water upstream, which sometimes involves the utility meter, so it helps to schedule it rather than do it mid-crisis.

A new ball valve closes in a quarter turn and is less likely to seize. If your house has no accessible shutoff near the entry point, adding one is an easy upgrade that pays for itself the first time something breaks.

Fixture Shutoffs Are Worth Knowing Too

Every sink and toilet should have its own stop valve. Those small valves let you fix a faucet or replace a toilet without closing the whole house. If any of them are stuck or leak, replace them while you are checking the main valve.

  • Check the valves under every sink and behind every toilet.
  • Turn each one gently to be sure it moves.
  • Replace any that drip, stick or show corrosion.
  • Keep the access clear, since a cabinet full of cleaning supplies slows an emergency.

Teaching Your Household

A shutoff only works if the people in the house know where it is. Walk everyone through it, including teenagers and anyone who house-sits, and tag the valve with a bright label. Put a flashlight nearby for dark basements and crawlspaces.

Write the location in your home records and on the inside of a cabinet door, next to your plumber's phone number. When water is pouring from a wall, no one wants to hunt.

For a rental, tell the tenants and the landlord where the main is. Shutting off water early is the single biggest factor in how much a burst pipe costs.

Key Takeaways

  • Find your main shutoff before you need it, and tag it.
  • Replace a stiff gate valve with a quarter-turn ball valve.
  • The meter and its valve belong to the utility, so use your house valve in an emergency.
  • Teach everyone in the house where it is and how it turns.

Questions About Main Shutoff Valves in Camas

Where is the main shutoff valve in a Camas home?

It is usually where the water line enters the house, such as a basement wall, garage wall or utility room, and some homes also have one near the water heater.

Who do I call if the street-side valve at the meter needs to be turned?

The meter and the valve on its street side belong to the utility, so ask the City of Camas water division.

How often should I exercise my main valve?

Turning it off and on once or twice a year keeps it from seizing, as long as it is not already stiff.

Should I replace a gate valve with a ball valve?

Many plumbers recommend it, because a ball valve closes in a quarter turn and is less likely to seize.
