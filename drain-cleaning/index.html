Backup in Progress? What to Do Before We Arrive

Stop using every fixture that drains into the blocked line. Running the dishwasher or flushing again only pushes more water toward the lowest opening in the house, usually a tub or a floor drain.

If one sink is slow and every other fixture works, the clog is local and close to the fixture. If several drains gurgle or back up together, the blockage is in the main line and the next step is a cable or a camera, not another bottle of chemical cleaner.

Skip the chemical drain opener

Caustic cleaners sit in a standing blockage and can burn through old pipe or hurt the person who works on it next. Tell us if you already used one before we start.

What We Use to Open Different Kinds of Clogs

Kitchen lines clog with grease and soap scum, bathroom lines with hair, and main lines with roots, wipes and years of scale. Each one calls for a different tool, which is why a single approach never fits every call.

  • Hand and drum augers for sink, tub and toilet clogs close to the fixture.
  • Main line cables with cutting heads for roots and packed debris.
  • Camera inspection when a line keeps clogging and we need to see why.
  • Hydro jetting when scale or grease has narrowed the pipe, covered on our hydro jetting page.

After the line is open we run water through it and check the other fixtures, so you know the clog is really gone and not just punched through.

Drains and the Camas STEP Sewer System

Many Camas properties connect to the city's STEP system, where waste flows from the house to a small tank near the street. The city owns the tank, the pump and the control panel, while the property owner maintains the pipe from the house to the tank.

That split matters for a backup. If your lines run slow, we clear and inspect the part you own. If the outside alarm panel is sounding, the tank is the city's side of the system, and the city asks that the lids stay uncovered with at least 1 inch of clearance. The city also says flushable wipes are not suitable for these systems and that additives should not be used.

Drain and vent layout in Washington follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), adopted with state amendments through WAC 51-56, which is why we check slope and venting when a line keeps clogging.

Drain Care That Keeps the Line Open

Hot water and a squirt of dish soap after greasy dishes helps, but grease still cools and sticks farther down the line. Wipe pans with a paper towel first, and use a hair catch in every tub and shower.

Wipes marked flushable do not break down like toilet paper, so they belong in the trash. If your lines are old or you have tree roots near the sewer, a camera visit through our sewer camera inspection service shows what is happening before the next backup does.

Drain Cleaning Questions From Camas Homeowners

Do you handle drain cleaning emergencies at night or on weekends in Camas?

Yes. The line is open 24 hours a day, every day. If a drain is backing up into your home, stop the water use and call (360) 371-6092, and we will talk through the safest next step.

Why does my drain keep clogging in the same spot?

A repeat clog usually points to something fixed in the pipe, like a sag, a root, a rough joint or scale. A camera look finds it, and a repair or a jetting visit then solves it instead of clearing it again and again.

Is it safe to use a store-bought drain cleaner first?

Chemical openers rarely clear a hard blockage and can burn skin or damage older pipe. If you already poured one in, tell the plumber so the line is handled with gloves and eye protection.

Can a clogged drain be a sewer problem?

Yes. When more than one fixture backs up, or the lowest drain in the house gurgles, the blockage is probably in the main line, and our sewer line cleaning work addresses it.

How do I know if my home is on the STEP system?

Look for a pair of green lids near the street and an alarm panel on the outside of the house. If you are unsure, ask the City of Camas which kind of sewer connection your address has. We also take drain calls in Washougal.
